So that all the peoples of the earth may know that we, the surviving remnant, for as long as we are strangers and sojourners in a land not our own,
are obliged, according to the true prophets and the custom of our fathers, which has the force of Torah,
to pray for the welfare of the kingdom that rules over us. They should also know that at a time like this in particular, when our iniquities have scattered us to the four winds of heaven, we must seek God's favor for the peace of all the inhabitants of the world, so that "nation shall not lift up sword against nation" [Isa 2:4].
I have thought it right to gather the scattered proofs, and the rulings for practice, that I have found and seen.
"And the heart of the discerning acquires knowledge" [Prov 18:15]. Now Jeremiah, in chapter 29, concerning Babylon, the beginning of our ruin, commanded the Jews as God's message, saying:
"Seek the peace of the city to which I have exiled you, and pray to the Lord on its behalf, for in its peace you shall have peace" [Jer 29:7].
And in the first chapter of the book of his disciple Baruch, which the Christians accept although it is not accepted among us,
it is set out that the part of the Jews who were first exiled with Jeconiah sent to the remnant that remained with the high priest in Jerusalem a collection of donations from all of them, with these words:

"We are sending you money with which to buy burnt offerings and incense. Pray for the life of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and for the life of Belshazzar his son, that their life may be like the days of heaven, and that we may serve them many days and they may show us kindness" [Bar 1:10–12].
In Ezra 6 we find how King Darius confirmed the decree of Cyrus, in which he commanded: "From the king's property let [the expenses] be given them day by day, that they may offer sacrifices of pleasing odor to the God of heaven and pray for the life of the king and his sons" [Ezra 6:8–10].
And there is no doubt that this order of his was carried out in full force, as Yosippon for the Romans [i.e., Josephus] attests in book 11, chapter 4.
In tractate Yoma, chapter Ba lo, and in Megillat Ta'anit, chapter 9, we find that the twenty-first of Kislev is the Day of Mount Gerizim, when the Samaritans asked Alexander of Macedon to destroy the House of our God because of the high place they had set up for themselves on Mount Gerizim [b. Yoma 69a].
And Simeon the Righteous said before him: "The place in which we pray for you and for your kingdom that it not be destroyed, shall these gentiles lead you astray into destroying it?"
And in the first book of the Hasmoneans, chapter 7, we find that when Demetrius king of the Greeks sent Nicanor his general against the Jews, at the instigation of the wicked Eliakim, who had sought to become high priest and had not attained it,
some of the priests and of the Sanhedrin went out to meet him at Mount Zion, and among their other words of supplication before him they showed him the sacrifices and burnt offerings they were offering for the welfare of the king [1 Macc 7:33].
Likewise in the second book of the Hasmoneans, chapter 3, and in Yosippon for the Romans in his separate book on the Hasmoneans, chapter 4, and [in Yosippon] for the Jews, chapter 16, book 3, it is written that when Heliodorus, general of Seleucus king of the Greeks, came at the instigation of Simon the Benjaminite, who likewise had incited the king to plunder the glorious wealth of the Sanctuary,
Onias the high priest, in pleading with him, said to him: "My lord general, surely you have no claim or cause to harm this place, in which day by day we pray for the king and his sons and his friends, that God may grant them lasting tranquility and ease." ⚠1

And Yosippon for the Hebrews, book 6, chapter 47, relates how the Jews in Asia sent Hyrcanus the priest and the princes of Judah letters and a gift, saying:
"Take what we have sent you for the honor of our God, and pray for the life of our lord Augustus, emperor of Rome, and the life of his colleague Mark Antony."
And Yedidyah the Alexandrian [Philo], in his last book, entitled On his embassy to Gaius, mentions that in the letter that Agrippa king of the Jews sent to the aforementioned emperor Gaius, to ask of him that he not place his image in the holy place, he wrote:
"Our lord king, do not the Jews make vows for the welfare of your reign, and offer gifts with burnt offerings, not only on the well-known festivals but also in the sacrifices that we offer daily to our God?"
Likewise Yosippon for the Romans, in his second book of the Wars, chapter 9, wrote that when Petronius, general of the aforementioned Gaius, sought to set up his statue in the Temple,
the Jews pleaded before him, saying: "Surely we offer day by day burnt offering and sacrifices to God for the welfare of the emperor and all the Roman people."
And in his second book Against Apion he wrote in general terms, saying:
"We Jews have always been accustomed to glorify and exalt all the emperors and the Roman people and to offer burnt offerings and sacrifices on their behalf,
not only with the communal sacrifices; rather, many times, apart from the sacrifices made for the congregation as a whole or for individuals in particular,
we offer special burnt offerings in honor of the emperors, which is not done for anyone else on earth."

And explicit is the saying of the Fathers of the World [Avot], chapter 3: "Pray for the welfare of the kingdom, for were it not for fear of it, each man would swallow his neighbor alive" [m. Avot 3:2].
What is more, not only for the kingdom ruling over us,
but also for the other kingdoms that in the days of our sovereignty were numbered among those who loved us, our fathers did not cease to make supplication.
As you will find in the first book of the Hasmoneans, chapter 12: when Jonathan the high priest made a covenant with the government of the Spartans, who said that since they were sons of Abraham they were found to be the bone and flesh of the Jews, he wrote to them thus:
"On the festivals we remember you at the sacrifices, as it befits us to remember our brothers" [1 Macc 12:11].
And still more than this: not only for our masters and friends,
but also for all the inhabitants of the world we pray to our God that He extend His kindness to them.
Do you not see that in that letter which Agrippa wrote to the emperor Gaius, as mentioned, it is written:

"The custom of the high priest on the Day of Atonement is to pray that all human beings in general may have a year of blessing, quiet and security."
Also Yedidyah himself, in his book entitled On the Universal Kingship, said: "The priests of the other nations pray for their own nation alone, ⚠2
but the high priest of the Jews seeks blessing and plenty not only for the human race alone but also for the other parts of the world and its elements."
And likewise in the [book] entitled On the Burnt Offerings he wrote, in his words: "Some of the sacrifices are made for our nation, and some for the entire human race, ⚠3
for indeed the two daily offerings with the meal offering and the libations are a thanksgiving on behalf of all human beings for the Creator's benefits, which He graciously bestows on them by day and by night."
Yosippon too, in the second [book] Against Apion, wrote: "We sacrifice, and pray at length, first for the inhabitants of the world in general, that they may be filled with every good and success,
and afterwards for ourselves in particular, for we know that prayer for the general good before the particular is very pleasing before our God."
You will find these words of his also in Eusebius of Caesarea, in his Preparation, book 8, chapter 2; take them to heart, for they are pleasant. ⚠4

Henceforth, intelligent reader, you will give honor to the name of our ancients for what they instituted in the prayers from the beginning of the year to its end:
"Reign over the whole world... and appear in the splendor... and let every creature understand...",
"to perfect the world under the kingdom of the Almighty... for to You every knee shall bend... and let them all accept the yoke of Your kingdom,"
and everything similar to these words, which for us take the place of "sacrifices of righteousness" [Ps 4:6].
And the sage Don Isaac [Abravanel] passed over them without mention in his book Nahalat Avot, chapter 4, in the thirteenth root on the matter of the resurrection, on the Mishnah's phrase "for everything is His"; ⚠5
for this reason it is fitting and acceptable that He should strive to bring all creatures back to Himself, as it says: "The Lord is good to all, and His mercies are over all His works" [Ps 145:9].
And in the daily prayer, three times a day, we pray: "and satisfy the world from Your blessings,"
for "surely God is good to Israel, to the pure of heart" [Ps 73:1] to walk in the ways of our God, who does not desire the death of anyone who dies [Ezek 18:32], but rather that the one who has turned away from Him forsake his thoughts and return to the Lord, and He will have mercy on him [Isa 55:7].

So too the custom found in some congregations, by way of additional reinforcement, of blessing their rulers and all their officials
is truly a custom of the pious of old, [fit] to "find favor and good understanding in the eyes of God and man" [Prov 3:4].
Also what is written in some prayers, to remove the dominion of arrogance and to uproot and break its kingdom, so that the spirit of impurity may pass from the earth —
we should truly praise it when the words are understood in this correct sense:
namely, that we ask for the recovery of the sick person through the removal of the sickness. For there is no doubt that arrogance, and the sin of haughtiness, is the evil inclination and the angel of death, which always crouches at the door of our heart [cf. Gen 4:7] to make us stumble,
and when it is subdued we shall humble ourselves and submit to God. See the verse "And the man who acts with arrogance" [Deut 17:12],
"the prophet has spoken it with arrogance" [Deut 18:22], and similar ones. And when it is far from us, shall we not long, as the hart for the water brooks [Ps 42:2], for the good deeds, in which is all the life of our spirit [cf. Isa 38:16]?
And this is like the word of the divine psalmist: "Let sins cease from the earth" [Ps 104:35],
which [the sages] of blessed memory praised, the sense being "sins" and not "sinners" [b. Berakhot 10a].

And you will find the like in the gentile sage Seneca, in his wise sayings, in these words: ⚠6
"It is most praiseworthy to uproot not the sinners but the sin and the transgression."
And that in the Holy Writings this usage is customary, attributing rank and dominion to sin and arrogance itself,
is an explicit verse in Ezekiel 7: "The rod has blossomed, arrogance has budded, violence has risen up" [Ezek 7:10–11],
and Jeremiah 50: "And arrogance shall stumble and fall" [Jer 50:32]. And in the words of our rabbis in Nedarim, chapter 3: "'A little city' [Eccl 9:14] — this is the body...,
'and a great king came against it' — this is the evil inclination" [b. Nedarim 32b]. And the Targum [renders] "If the spirit of the ruler rises up against you" [Eccl 10:4] as "if the spirit of the evil inclination rules over you."
It may also be that from this usage came what the lawgiver of the Christians, who was a Jew, wrote in his Epistle to the Romans, chapter 6, in these words:
"Let not sin therefore reign in your body, which is destined for death" [Rom 6:12].

Indeed, only if a remnant of the nations is found on earth can what we have hoped for the remnant of our people be established, according to the promise in Daniel 7:
"And to him shall be given dominion and kingship, and all peoples, nations and tongues shall serve him" [Dan 7:14, cf. 7:27].
And since all that we have mentioned is entirely true, and it is known that all the rest of the remnant left among us is scattered and dispersed from one end of the world to the other,
there is surely no way for our hearts to incline toward the success of one kingdom over another,
for woe to people in every one that falls; and the blood of all of them is ours, red and precious in the eyes of our God. ⚠7
Therefore it is a commandment to heed the words of the sages and the "counsels from afar" of the prophets, "faithfulness firm" [Isa 25:1], to raise on behalf of all the kingdoms song and prayer,
that the Lord may remove from their hearts all rivalry and enmity and give peace in the land; for surely in their peace He will ordain peace for us too [cf. Isa 26:12],
and all will see and give thanks when in His kindness He exalts our horn and speedily causes the shoot of David His servant to sprout.

And we have already noted, near the end of chapter 5 of Ma'amar 1, on the ground of the interpretation of the Seventy, Yedidyah and Yosippon of the verse "You shall not revile God [elohim]" [Exod 22:27],
that in everything pertaining to matters of faith and divinity "it is an honor for a man to keep aloof from strife" [Prov 20:3], close to the saying
"Do not provoke them, and take great care" [Deut 2:4–5]; for thereby our tents shall be at ease, until God the Savior shall desire us.
