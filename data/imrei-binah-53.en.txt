Since in the preceding chapters I have mentioned some of the high priests according to the opinion of Josephus in the Antiquities,
I saw fit to copy here what he wrote on the sum of them all, as it is found in his Book 20, end of chapter 8. ⚠1
But first I must say that these words of his are certainly not free of corruptions, as you will see in the marginal gloss beside this page on some clear places; and likewise in the rest of the book, there is no doubt that in some part of it, for whatever reason, hands have tampered with it, as I also wrote in chapter 19 concerning the Hebrew [Josippon] that we have;
therefore I will not refrain from saying about it what Maimonides of blessed memory said in his medical book entitled Pirkei Moshe [the Medical Aphorisms], at the beginning of chapter 25, in which he collected a number of errors that, in his opinion, are found in the books of Galen.
But since the works [of Galen] that reached him were translated from Greek into Arabic, he said, in these words:
"It is possible that it was not Galen who erred, but the translator, or I the reader." And here we may add the scribes or the printers.
In any case this chapter is no idle matter; therefore I have set it before you.
These are his words: "It is fitting for these writings that we make known concerning the high priests who was the first of them all,
and which men may be granted this dignity, and how many there were up to the end of the aforementioned war.

The first, then, who served as priest to God was Aaron, the brother of Moses.
And after his death his sons served as priests, for his seed after him had the covenant of an everlasting priesthood,
and so the order continued, that none should serve as priest to God except the seed of Aaron — not even kings of another lineage.
Now from this Aaron until that Phinehas who, in the days of the aforementioned war, was proclaimed high priest by some of the people, there were eighty-three high priests,
namely, thirteen of them from the day on which, by the word of God, Moses set up the Tabernacle in the wilderness until Solomon built the Temple.
For in the early days a new high priest was not chosen until the death of his predecessor,
but in the course of time they corrupted the matter, [appointing] while the first was still alive someone who would rise in his stead in the holy place.
And since the aforementioned thirteen were descendants of one or the other of Aaron's two sons, they continued in their former dignity,
and the governance of the people in their days was under the hand of the judges. Afterwards it passed into the hand of the universal kings, that is, monarchs, such as Saul and David, ⚠2
and finally into the hand of particular kings. From the day the children of Israel went out of Egypt through Moses until the beginning of Solomon's building, 612 years elapsed.

And after the aforementioned thirteen high priests there were eighteen other high priests, from the beginning of Solomon's building until the first destruction by Nebuchadnezzar;
and Jehozadak, who was then high priest, went into exile with the rest of the people of the land. This priesthood of theirs lasted 466 years, 6 months and 10 days,
and the governance of the people was then in the hands of the kings. After the seventy years of our exile,
Cyrus, who freely chose to send the children of Israel free to their land,
also gave them permission to renew the building; and Jeshua son of Jehozadak was then high priest, for his father had died.
And this Jeshua and his descendants after him, fifteen generations, continued in this priesthood of theirs and sustained the people of the land for 413 years, until Antiochus Eupator arose.
Then this Antiochus and his general Lysias seized Onias the high priest, who was also called Menelaus, and near the city of Beroea took from him his life and his priesthood; ⚠3
they also barred Onias his son from any further share in that dignity, and appointed in his stead Jakim [Alcimus], who was indeed of the seed of Aaron, but not of the former family.
Therefore Onias son of Onias, the slain high priest, who was likewise called by his father's name,
fled to Egypt, and since he found favor in the eyes of Ptolemy and Cleopatra his wife, he obtained [permission] to erect in Alexandria an altar in the likeness of the one in Jerusalem, of which we have spoken elsewhere.

And after the priesthood of this Jakim had lasted three years and he died, no one served as priest in his stead for seven years, during which the high priesthood ceased from the city and the sanctuary.
When the Hasmoneans reigned, who rebelled against the Greeks and drove them out, the congregation of the Jews chose Jonathan as high priest; and after he had served as priest seven years, Trypho rose against him and killed him, and Simon his brother served as priest in his stead.
And when he too died, for his son-in-law "destroyed the tree with its fruit" [cf. Jer 11:19], Hyrcanus his son rose in his place for thirty-one years.
And since he was old and advanced in years, he gave the priesthood to Judah the Hasmonean, who by another name was called Aristobulus.
And after this Judah had served as priest and reigned one year,
his brother, called Alexander, rose in his stead to the priesthood and kingship; he served as priest and reigned twenty-seven years, and at his death he granted his wife that she should choose the high priest as she wished.
And she gave the dignity to Hyrcanus her son; and after she had continued in her reign nine years, she was gathered to her people.
Then Hyrcanus could not continue in his priesthood, for Aristobulus his brother rose against him after their mother's death and removed him from it, to be like the rest of the priests, and took for himself priesthood and kingship together.

After three years and three months, while he was in the aforementioned dignities, Pompey captured Jerusalem and sent him with his sons bound in chains to Rome,
and restored Hyrcanus to his priesthood and also to the dignity of kingship, but on condition that he might not set the crown upon his head;
and so, besides the nine years we mentioned, he served as priest another twenty-three years.
After this, Barzapharnes and Pacorus, princes of the Parthians, crossed the river Euphrates, made war on Hyrcanus, captured him alive and brought him to their lands, and set up Antigonus son of Aristobulus as king. ⚠4
At the end of three years and three months Herod, with the help of the general Mark Antony, killed Antigonus near Antioch. ⚠5
And this Herod, who by the help of the Romans reigned over the Jews by force,
did not wish high priests to arise any longer from the seed of the Hasmonean, and gave that dignity to common priests.
True, for a time he appointed as priest Aristobulus, grandson of Hyrcanus who had been captured by the Parthians and brother of his wife Mariamme, for so the people requested in memory of Hyrcanus his grandfather;
but after some time, fearing lest the people incline after Hyrcanus, he contrived his death by guile while he was swimming in the water near Jericho, as we related above,
and from then on he did not wish any descendant of the Hasmonean to serve as priest again.

Likewise after Herod's death, Archelaus his son, who rose in his stead, held to his father's way,
and also the Roman governors who were with him agreed not to allow any more of the seed of the Hasmoneans to approach the sanctuary in the high priesthood."
