However, Eusebius the Christian, of the city of Caesarea, in his Chronicle (fol. 52 of his tables),
wrote that the kings of Persia, who began (19) in the 55th Olympiad, were these fourteen: ⚠1
(1) Cyrus the Persian, thirty years. (2) Cambyses, eight years.
And he wrote of him that, in the opinion of some, he is called Nebuchadnezzar among the Jews, and that the episode of Judith took place in his days.
(3) The two Magi brothers, seven months; in the totals of years they are counted as one year.
(4) Darius, thirty-six years. In his second year Zerubbabel returned to build the Temple, and it was completed in his sixth year,
for although they had already begun it in the first year of Cyrus, the adversaries held it up for forty years.
(5) Xerxes, son of Darius, twenty years. (6) Artabanus the Persian, seven months, counted as one year.

(7) Artaxerxes, surnamed Longimanus, that is, 'long of hand', forty years.
Many say that the episode of Esther took place in his days,
but 'my heart does not think so' [cf. Isa 10:7] (says Eusebius), for Ezra and Nehemiah, who recorded his acts, would not have passed over that episode in silence.
(8) Artaxerxes [sic] the Persian, two months. (9) Sogdianus, seven [sic: eight] months. ⚠2
(10) Darius, surnamed Nothus, nineteen years. (11) Artaxerxes the Persian, called Mnemon, son of Darius and Parysatis, forty years,
and in his days the episode of Esther took place. (12) Artaxerxes, also called Ochus, twenty-six years.
(13) Arses, son of Ochus, four years. (14) Darius the last, son of Arsames, six years.
The high priests he likewise recorded according to the same number and order as they are listed by Yedidyah [Philo] and Yosippon, mentioned above, ⚠3
namely Jeshua, Joiakim, Eliashib, Joiada, Jonathan and Jaddua, Onias, Simeon the Just, Eleazar his brother, Manasseh the uncle of the aforementioned Eleazar.
All this is from the words of Eusebius. Further, 'to add to the number of men' [i.e., of witnesses], I will not fail to inform you that the ancient writer Quintus Curtius, in the second book of his work devoted to the history of Alexander of Macedon, wrote as follows: ⚠4

'Darius son of Arsames, who fell into the hands of Alexander, was the fourteenth king from Cyrus.'
Likewise the translator for the Christians [Jerome], in his commentary on the book of Daniel, on the verse 'Behold, there shall stand up yet three kings in Persia' [Dan 11:2], wrote as follows: ⚠5
'It is among the well-known facts that require no proof that from Cyrus to the aforementioned Darius there were fourteen kings of Media and Persia.'
This is what I saw fit to glean 'among the sheaves' [cf. Ruth 2:15] of the writers mentioned.
And now, discerning reader, 'pay attention and hear' [Deut 27:9] what I shall say to you in the chapters to come, and may our God be with us.
