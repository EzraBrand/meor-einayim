Among the things whose truth we ought to establish while we are still discussing the Era of Contracts,
and this for a reason that will become clear to you as these chapters proceed,
is that the Era of Contracts as we reckon it is correct and accepted by everyone,
for among all the scholars of the gentiles and of the Jews there is no one who counts in it a substantial number [of years] more or less.
Consider what the Christian Samotio wrote in his tables: that the beginning of Alexander's reign, by the astronomers' reckoning, was in the first year of the 114th Olympiad, which was 430 years after the founding of Rome and 323 before the Christian era; ⚠1
and in the sixth year of his reign he captured Darius, so that from this universal rule of his to the beginning of the Christian era, as appears from there, 315 years elapsed. ⚠2
Add to these, then, the 1571 years they currently count, and the total will be 1889;
subtract from them Alexander's last six years, which are "the six years that he reigned in Elam," which we do not count, as has been said [cf. b. Avodah Zarah 10a],
and the remaining sum will be 1883, the very same total counted for the Era of Contracts in our tables.

That is why you find two reckonings in them: 1889 for the beginning of the Greek kingdom, that is, their universal rule, which began in Alexander's sixth year when he subdued Darius;
and 1883 for the Era of Contracts, which is the "kingdom of the Greeks" mentioned by our Rabbis, beginning from the time his four servants reigned after him, as we have mentioned.
Our own eyes see that this reckoning hits the mark to a hair's breadth,
for although Eusebius, Abulogilio and other writers have Alexander's reign begin from the first year of the 111th Olympiad, ⚠3
so that the starting point of the one stands beside the root of the other with a difference of twelve years between them,
it is evident that our reckoning mentioned above is founded on the astronomers' system already mentioned: if that system stands, ours stands; and if it is pushed back those few years, ours too is pushed back correspondingly.
In any case, a difference of twelve years is not something we need to attend to at present.
And I had the delight that the Lord has this very day brought into my hands a copy of a letter that the Gaon Rav Sherira, of blessed memory, wrote to the holy community of Kairouan, not the one the author of the Arukh cited under the entry Abaye but another, seven times as long, ⚠4
which begins by resolving this difficulty: why Rabbi, the compiler of the Mishnah, climbs upward in the laws of the commandments, as if casting the burden of most of them upon the sages of later generations;
and which goes on to set out the chain of tradition from its beginning down to his own days. Among its other statements are these:

"In the days of Rabbi, Rav went down to Babylonia, in the year 530 of the Greek era which we customarily use; and in the year 570 R. Yohanan died." ⚠5
And after stating, according to the said Greek era, many dates at which various Amoraim lived, he wrote of Rav Ashi: "and he died in the year 738."
He likewise continued the sequence through the Savoraic rabbis and the generations of the Geonim, of blessed memory, and related how our master Saadia was appointed Gaon in the year 1239 of the said era.
The conclusion of his words is this: "And in the year 1279 I was appointed to the gaonate, and we appointed this son of ours [as] av bet din some two years ago; may it be [God's] will, etc."
"Written in the year 1298 of the Era of Contracts in the city of Peroz Shabur."
This, according to what he says there, lies on the border of Nehardea in the land of Babylonia,
and about ninety thousand Jews lived there, apart from the gentiles of the land.
As for the dates of the Amoraim that our master Samson of Chinon wrote in his Sefer Keritut, part "Yemot Olam," gate 2, which are likewise given in the Era of Contracts,
there is no doubt that he copied them from this letter. Moreover, this reckoning of the year 1279 of the Era of Contracts, in which Rav Sherira was appointed, as stated,
agrees with the total of 4725 from Creation that Rabad ha-Levi wrote concerning him in his Sefer ha-Kabbalah, [under] the seventh generation of the Geonim, may their souls rest. ⚠6

Similarly, regarding the year 530 of the Era of Contracts in which Rabbi compiled the Mishnah,
the sage [author of the] Kuzari agreed, near the end of Discourse 3, in these words:
"Rabbi compiled the Mishnah in the year 530 of the Era of Contracts, which is the year 150 after the destruction of the Second [Temple], 530 [years] after the cessation of prophecy." End of quotation.
Likewise the great luminary Maimonides, of blessed memory, in chapter 11 of the Laws of Sanctification of the New Moon and chapter 10 of the Laws of the Sabbatical Year, wrote in a manner consistent with the said Era of Contracts,
for he said: "This year, which is 1107 after the destruction, which is 1487 of the Era of Contracts, which is 4936 from Creation, is, etc."
So too R. Levi ibn Habib, in his responsum §143, where he set out in tables all the totals of years from Creation, wrote as follows: ⚠7
"The Era of Contracts began at the end of 40 years from when the Temple was built." ⚠8
Thus regarding the said Era of Contracts all writers, whether of Israel or of the nations of the world, speak with one voice; there is no divergence among them "that a child could write down" [cf. Isa 10:19].
