Something we should not pass over in silence regarding the "six thousand years" we have mentioned: these statements about the destruction of the world after six thousand years, however we understand that destruction and in whatever way we picture it,
hold only for one who believes that this tanna of the school of Elijah was touched by a gleam of prophecy and spoke the truth.
But according to the view of the master, [author of the] Guide, II, chapters 27, 28 and 29, which also seems to be his view in Hilkhot Teshuvah, end of chapter 8,
and likewise according to Yedidyah the Alexandrian in his book entitled That the World Is Indestructible, and others who philosophized among our people and also among the Christians, ⚠1
this is a fabrication and they did not uphold it. For they said that its Maker may, if He wishes, bring His sword upon it, or He may preserve it so that it lives before Him for length of days, forever and ever,
and that reason inclines rather to judge that He, may He be blessed, desires its continued existence.
Some verses also indicate as much, such as "He established the earth upon its foundations" etc. [Ps 104:5], and "You, O Lord, are enthroned forever; Your throne endures from generation to generation" [Lam 5:19]. I will add a proof of my own in support of their view: the angelic hosts of Scripture cry out abroad in the Torah, "to those who keep His commandments, to a thousand generations" [Deut 7:9],
quite apart from what is written in the Ten Commandments, "showing kindness to thousands" [Exod 20:6], which means thousands of generations,
as it was understood in the Yerushalmi and the Bavli of Sotah, chapter Ke-shem, where they said: one who acts from fear [is rewarded] to a thousand generations, from love to thousands [of generations];
and likewise in the Writings there is an explicit verse: "the word He commanded to a thousand generations" [Ps 105:8].

Now, even without regard to the words of Xenophon the Greek in his book On Equivocal Names, that according to the Egyptians a generation is thirty years, ⚠2
according to the Greeks it is twenty-five, and for the Romans, as appears from their poet Ovid in the twelfth book of the Metamorphoses, it is a hundred years; ⚠3
you of this generation, see what Ibn Ezra wrote on the verse "and the fourth generation" etc. [Gen 15:16],
that according to the usage of our holy language a generation (dor) is the time a person dwells (yador) in the world.
Kimhi too wrote thus under the root dur, although, since the lifetimes of father and son overlap, it suffices in reckoning the years to count the father until offspring is raised up to him.
And so you will understand from the words of Maimonides of blessed memory in his commentary on Mishnah Eduyot, chapter 2, on the passage "the number of generations before him,"
where he said: after so-and-so many generations of men, one begetting another, the thing he foretold will come to pass.
End quote. Be that as it may: a thousand generations, all the more so thousands, would exceed the years of the period of Saturn beyond reckoning.
I know that the masters of the true [i.e. kabbalistic] wisdom understood a generation as fifty years, and they explain the verse "to a thousand generations" as referring to the last Jubilee, which in their view comes at the end of fifty thousand years,
as R. Todros ha-Levi wrote in his book Otzar ha-Kavod on the aforementioned saying of the six thousand years.
But according to the words of [the Sages] of blessed memory in the aforementioned Yerushalmi and Bavli of Sotah, on the verse "showing kindness to thousands," meaning thousands of generations,
this exceeds that Jubilee, unless the cycles continue forever according to the view we shall mention below. It also seems possible to bring proof for this view from what is written in Daniel 2 concerning the kingdom destined to come after the four kingdoms are subdued: "And in the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed" etc. [Dan 2:44],
and in chapter 8 he says likewise of it, "and all peoples, nations and tongues shall serve him" etc., up to "for ever and for ever and ever" [Dan 7:14, 18], ⚠4
and these words can by no means be referred to the world of souls, for there will be no successive generations and different kingdoms there,
but necessarily to this world. The sage Recanati already touched on this in parashat Be-har Sinai, citing some later kabbalists who said it troubled them that the days of the Messiah for Israel should be so short a time, less than a thousand years,
whereas by rights the days of tranquility ought to be a thousand times longer than the days of affliction we have endured for the sanctification of the Lord; see there.

And even granting the aforementioned variation [in the length of a generation] according to Ovid (in the Mantua and Berlin editions it reads "for certain", ⚠5
but one should pay attention to this variation, and Ovid's variation requires investigation),
other opinions are found. For the divines among the Christian scholars divided the years of the world, which is a macroanthropos, not into six equal bounds of a thousand years each, as our Sages say,
but into six periods which they called aetates, each of limited duration but one long and another short,
like the different periods of the life of man, who is a microcosm; and in their wake there will doubtless be the variation we have mentioned.
The sixth period, which by their reckoning began with the Nativity, is the one likened to old age, which has no fixed number of years; rather, there is an old man whose old age lasts as long as most of his earlier days,
and after the sixth aetas will come the period likened to the Sabbath in the sum of the days of the week.
Accordingly, on their view, the aforementioned sixth period, the old age of the world, may exceed the total of six thousand years by far more than we reckoned,
as you will see all this in their sage Augustine, at the end of the last chapter of his City [of God], and Aquinas in the fourth [book] of his commentary on the Book of Sentences, distinction 43, article 1, and distinction 47, question 1, article 1, ⚠6
and Contractus, printed after the tables of Eusebius at the beginning of his chronicle, expanded on this discourse more than they. ⚠7

The divine philosopher Plato has another view on this, namely belief in cycles,
for in the book of the Statesman he wrote, and these are his words: Behold, God, may He be blessed, at one time gathers the world to Himself, and at another time slackens His hand and lets it go, when He sees that time has revolved through the measures sufficient until then. ⚠8
In the Timaeus too, here a little, there a little, he wrote similar things, which you will find collected in Eusebius of Caesarea, book 11 of his Preparation, chapter 17,
where he says: Behold, time was made together with the heavens, so that together they might likewise be dissolved; ⚠9
and shortly after you will find this passage, which imagines that the supreme God spoke to the host of heaven, saying: Behold, you are the work of My hands, and of yourselves you are destined to dissolution;
as we shall mention again shortly. Be that as it may: first I would alert you not to err, as many do, in believing that this Elijah who teaches us these words was the Tishbite who ascended to heaven;
rather he is the tanna listed in Maimonides' introduction to Zera'im next to Honi ha-Me'aggel, at the end of chapter 7; and in chapter Mi she-hayah you will find: "Tanna de-Vei Eliyahu: R. Natan says, the entire inhabited world lies beneath one star" etc. [b. Pesahim 94a].
After that, I say, may the aforementioned master, [author of the] Guide, forgive us: we will not heed his words in this matter,
for besides what we mentioned, that what is meant by the word "desolate" is not absolute destruction but a change for the better in the manner God shall desire,
our eyes see the masters of the true wisdom holding to the view of the tanna of the school of Elijah,
and it is not an isolated saying in a single form, as he of blessed memory characterized it there in chapter 29.

For among other places you will find at the end of tractate Tamid: "On the Sabbath they would say 'A psalm, a song for the Sabbath day' — for the time to come, for the day that is wholly Sabbath and rest for life everlasting" [m. Tamid 7:4],
and they said: "This tanna holds like the one who said the world will exist for six thousand years" etc. [b. Rosh Hashanah 31a].
Likewise in the Zohar, Bereshit, on the verse "And the woman saw," it is written: "Come and see, this Shemittah" etc., "and because it was from the left side it has no endurance until the time of the seventh millennium" etc.;
and likewise the other saying that the sage Don Isaac [Abravanel] cited in Ma'ayan 11, Tamar 10, from Zohar Bereshit (though I have not found it in our printed text): "R. Eliezer and R. Akiva were walking" etc., up to "and he that is left in Jerusalem."
The true kabbalist Nahmanides of blessed memory also wrote thus in his commentary on the verse "which God created to make" [Gen 2:3].
And Ibn Ezra on the verse "while the earth remains" [Gen 8:22] wrote that it is a sign that it has a fixed end,
and in parashat Be-har Sinai he wrote: "The meaning of 'a Sabbath unto the Lord' is like the Sabbath day, and the secret of the days of the world is hinted at in this place" [Ibn Ezra on Lev 25:2].
Likewise you will see Rabad objecting to the master of blessed memory at the end of chapter 8 of Hilkhot Teshuvah, although the sages of Lunel answered on his behalf, as appears from what is noted on it there.
Rashba too, in his Responsa no. 9, holds this view as being received tradition among our Sages,
and above them all the sage Recanati, in parashat Be-har Sinai, wrote at length against Maimonides of blessed memory and upheld the aforementioned saying, along with many sayings of [the Sages] of blessed memory on this subject based on Holy Scripture; see there.

And although the chief of the Christian scholars, in his City [of God], book 12, chapter 13, mocked Plato, who says that existence revolves round and round through its stages at the end of limited cycles, as mentioned,
just as he also mocked, in chapter 20 of the same, those who believe in the transmigration of souls, as his words were explained by the commentator Vives;
behold, the sage Messer Leone, author of the Dialogues of Philo and Sophia, in the third part of it, cited Plato's view that the heavens too are destined to dissolution, and expounded at great length that the aforementioned cycles are an ancient tradition from Enoch and Noah, ⚠10
and that the Torah commandments that recur in sevens — in days, the weeks of the Omer, the months of the year, the years of the Shemittah, and the Shemittot of the Jubilee —
are all an indication and faithful testimony to the circuits of six thousand years, up to forty-nine thousand, in the periods of the Shemittot and the Jubilee.
We have already mentioned the words of the sage Ibn Ezra about "the secret of the days of the world," on which you may read the book Mekor Hayyim in the aforementioned parashat Be-har Sinai. ⚠11
And the matter follows a fortiori from what we find in the name of R. Meir in the Yerushalmi of Shabbat, in these words:
"Once every seven years the Holy One, blessed be He, renews His world." Yedidyah the Alexandrian too, in his book On the Creation of the World, among the many praises he mentions of the number seven,
wrote that according to Solon, lawgiver of the Greeks, and Hippocrates, chief of physicians, the sum of man's seventy years changes for him into ten stages, which he mentioned there in a metrical poem of pleasing sense; ⚠12
how much more, then, the world as a whole, which will not escape a wondrous change proper to it, as our Sages say.

And in the Zohar, Va-yikra, on the verse "if a sacrifice of peace offerings," they said, in these words:
"Come and see: when the Holy One, blessed be He, created the world, He created seven firmaments above, seven earths below, seven seas, seven rivers, seven days, seven weeks, seven years, seven times seven thousand years that the world exists,
and the Holy One, blessed be He, is in the seventh of all." Truly, we will not refrain from saying that, both regarding this end and regarding the end of our exile, of which we spoke in the previous chapter, and likewise regarding all such deep and hidden matters,
there is no doubt that the best course is not to delve deep or soar high in investigation and striving of spirit over it,
but to cast our burden upon the will of our Father in heaven, whom we know to be wise, willing to do good, and great in power.
And just as the sage Socrates used to say (according to what Xenophon the Greek wrote in his name in the fourth book of his work entitled On the Deeds and Sayings of Socrates), together with his saying that every science and study has a limit and bound which it befits a man to reach and not pass beyond, ⚠13
he likewise used to say and teach all men not to toil to know the nature of the dispositions of the heavens, their constellations and their laws,
and especially not to seek to know what God wishes to do in His world at one time rather than another,
for this is an apprehension most difficult and alien to human nature, and also a great evil in the eyes of God,
that a mortal should dare seek to have revealed by Him what His wisdom decreed to conceal and to keep hidden from our eyes.
End of his words. And that old man was consistent with his own view, for you will find of him how, when the spirit of wisdom began to move him and he found that the ancients had applied themselves to investigating the secrets of natural and divine matters,
he turned from their path, saying: what is above us is no concern of ours,
and he turned to inquiry into human virtues and the ordering of society, which is necessary for the inhabitants of this transient world. And clearly the wisest of all men said: "As you do not know" etc., "so you do not know the work of God" [Eccl 11:5]. Indeed, even of those who know — [they know] some things, and not all things;
one of the greatest of the sages said to a man of his rank, in chapter Ha-zorek, and in another connection in the Yerushalmi, chapter Ke-shem:
"You will be called to account, for the Torah covered it and you reveal it," as we mentioned in chapters 15 and 42.
And the expounders of precious things — or "weighty things," as in Rashi's reading — said in chapter Arvei Pesahim: "What is 'and for him that covers ancient' (u-li-mekhaseh atik)? For one who covers the things that the Ancient of Days covered" [b. Pesahim 119a].
And if this is so for one who tells the truth, what shall be the judgment of the prophet with whom dreams speak falsehood?

Let us therefore do and heed the good counsel that they said in the name of Ben Sira in chapter Ein dorshin: "Do not inquire into what is too wondrous for you" [b. Hagigah 13a], as you will find it at length in chapter 3 of his book, which the Christians call Ecclesiasticus.
And the Lord God, who is truth, will accomplish on our behalf, and His purpose will prosper in our hands. Amen. [End of] the fourth treatise.
