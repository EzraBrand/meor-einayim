However, Metasthenes the Persian, in his book On the Order of Times (a short work recently translated into the Roman tongue together with the books of Berosus the Chaldean, and commented on by the Christian scholar Annius), wrote as follows: ⚠1
"Those who wish to write about the times would do well not to follow in them what is commonly heard among the multitude, or the opinion of some single individual of old.
For if that is their approach, as is the custom of the Greeks, they will surely, like them, deceive both themselves and others alike, and walk in darkness all their days.
Truly, we shall not err if we seek to know and pursue the chronicles that exist for the kingdoms that held sway in the world,
and reject whatever goes beyond them, from any writer whatever, as something not to be trusted by us. ⚠2
For in them the course of the times and the succession of the kings are divided faithfully and clearly, since their reigns are manifest and known.
Nor (a) are all the writers from these kingdoms to be accepted; only the priests of that kingdom are to be believed, who possessed the truth of the public and the hidden [records] of all its events, such as the man Berosus.
For he, being a Chaldean, wrote all the times of the Assyrians in the way he found them, divided in order, in the ancient records.
Therefore we Persians go forth following in his footsteps alone.

He, then, wrote that two hundred and forty-nine years before Ninus
three kings who were called gods reigned in the world. (b) The first of them, who reigned over it as a whole, was Ogyges (thus they called Noah), ruler over the earth when the waters prevailed.
After him Saturnus (thus they called Nimrod), who founded the land of Babylon and reigned in it fifty-six years.
After him Belus Jupiter his son, sixty-two; after him Ninus his son, who was the first of the monarchs, fifty-two.
Semiramis, forty-two; Zameis [Ninyas], thirty-eight; Arius, thirty; ⚠3
Aralius, forty; Xerxes Baleus, thirty; Armatritis, thirty-eight;
Belochus the First, thirty-five; Baleus the Second, forty-two;
Altadas, thirty-two; Mamythus, thirty; Machaleus, thirty; Sphaerus, twenty;
Mamylus, thirty; Sparetus, forty; Ascatades, forty; Amyntes, forty-five;

Belochus the Second, twenty-five; Belopares, thirty;
Lamprides, thirty-two; Sosares, forty; Panyas, thirty. ⚠4
Dercylus, forty; Eupalmes, thirty-eight; Laosthenes, forty-five;
Pertiades, thirty; Ophrataeus, twenty; Ophratanes, fifty;
Acrazanes, forty-two; Tonos Concoleros, fifteen,
whom the Greeks call Sardanapalus.' Thus far Berosus.
Now we who follow him have relied on no writer,
but only on what is found in the public records in the archive of books at Shushan.

And from the chronicles that the Persians have it is clear that there was also a Belochus the Third,
who was general of Sardanapalus' army and secretly sought his ruin.
This Belochus divided the universal kingdom with Arbaces, who was then king of Media,
on this condition: that he, Belochus, would reign over Babylon and Arbaces over Media and Persia.
And so, after Sardanapalus killed [his household] and also cast himself into the fire, the kingdom was divided into two parts. ⚠5
Over Media these reigned three hundred and four years:
Arbaces, twenty-eight; Mandanes, fifty; Sosarmus, thirty;
Artycarmin, fifty; Arbianes, twenty-two; Arsaeus, forty;
Artynes, twenty-two; Astibaras with his son Apanda, twenty;
Apanda alone, thirty; Cyrus and Darius, after they prevailed over this Apanda, thirty-six.
For before the whole kingdom returned [to them], they reigned together in Persia six years,
and afterwards they appointed the son Cambyses over Persia for another six years, during which they fought with Tomyris, queen of the Scythians (c).

In the sixth year they were summoned by the Babylonians and slew Belshazzar.
Then the whole kingdom returned to the Persians. And for the same number of years the kingdom of Babylon likewise continued while the realm was divided:
Phul Belochus, first, forty-eight; Phul Assar, twenty-five;
Salmanassar, seventeen; Sennacherib, seven; Esarhaddon, ten;
Merodach, fifty-two; Ben Merodach, twenty-one; Nebuchadnezzar (d) the First, thirty-five;
Nebuchadnezzar the Great, forty-five; Amilianus, i.e. Evil-merodach, thirty;
Rigassar, his first son, three; Labassardach, his second son, six;
Belshazzar, his third son, five. After Belshazzar was slain, Cyrus and Darius reigned together two years;
afterwards Cyrus alone, twenty-two; Ahasuerus Artaxerxes the First, son of Darius, twenty.
He executed judgments on the people of Tamarica, who by guile had delivered his uncle into the hand of Queen Tomyris. ⚠6

This Ahasuerus had two sons, Cyrus Artabanus and Darius (e) Longimanus,
and they contended with each other over the kingship for seven months; in the seventh month Darius Longimanus prevailed over his brother and reigned thirty-seven years.
After him Darius Nothus his son, nineteen. After him Darius Mnemon, who is Artaxerxes the Great, fifty-five.
After him Artaxerxes Ochus, twenty-six. And in our own time Arses reigned four years, ⚠7
the last Darius, six; Alexander of Macedon, who brought the universal kingdom to the Greeks, twelve;
Seleucus Nicanor, who is now in his thirtieth year, is king over all Asia and Syria." Thus far the words of Metasthenes.
