Since I have often been in discussion with many people about the count of years that we reckon from the Creation, and they insist on their view that it is ancient and has been in use in Israel from the day we became God's treasured people,
for the sake of the preceding chapter, and for any further clarification we may need,
I will show you, my brother reader; hear me, this is what I have seen [cf. Job 15:17]: if you ask of the former days [Deut 4:32], it will become clear to you that the count from Creation has been our custom only since long after the closing of the Gemara,
or at most since the time of its closing and onward. For at first they used other starting points, differing from one another,
according to the circumstances of the time and the good or ill fortunes that befell our people in ancient years.
Consider first that, although the total of the years that passed from Creation to the time of the giving [of the Torah] is known from the course of the Torah's narratives,
the Torah does not attribute any narrative to a fixed era except the Exodus of Israel from Egypt, as will be explained shortly, and not to Creation, as it might have done.
Even in the account of Creation itself, instead of saying "In the year 2448 [before the present] God created the heavens" etc., it simply says "In the beginning" etc. [Gen 1:1],
which Onkelos rendered be-qadmin and Jonathan b. Uzziel min avla, just as [the latter] rendered "his tent at the beginning" [Gen 13:3] min avla,
and "your judges as at the first" [Isa 1:26] kad min avla, and "shall return to their former state" [Ezek 16:55] le-avilhen.
From these it is clear that, in the view of both, the word bereshit in its plain sense (apart from the hidden mysteries of the Merciful One in it) means "at first," "in the beginning," "in former times,"
that is to say: in the ancient time, in days of old, at the beginning of existence, God created, etc.,
as the Psalmist says (Psalm 102), "Of old You laid the foundation of the earth" [Ps 102:26], which Jonathan rendered min sheruya,
like "Formerly in Israel" [Ruth 4:7]. Thus we have no need for the explanation of the Master, the Guide [Maimonides], Part II chapter 30, "in a beginning,"
nor for the precise interpretation of the sage Sforno, "in the first indivisible instant."

I rejoiced that God brought into my hands a copy of the Guide in which the greatest scholar of his generation, R. Baruch of Peschiera, had studied, and I saw in his handwriting around the aforementioned chapter 30 of Part II that he cited the Targum of Jonathan b. Uzziel and its interpretation as stated, ⚠1
a sign that he accepted it more than what is written in the book.
After these things, see what [the Sages] of blessed memory wrote in the Jerusalem Talmud, Rosh Hashanah chapter 1, in these words:
"Whence do we know that one counts from the Exodus from Egypt? For it says, 'In the third month after the going out' [Exod 19:1].
I know this only for that time; whence for afterward? Scripture says, 'In the fortieth year after the going out' etc. [Num 33:38].
I know this only for the moment; whence for future generations? Scripture says, 'And it came to pass in the four hundred and eightieth year after the going out' etc. [1 Kgs 6:1].
Once the Temple was built they began to count from its building, as it says, 'And it came to pass at the end of twenty years, in which Solomon had built' etc. [1 Kgs 9:10].
When they were not worthy to count from its building, they began to count from its destruction, as it says, 'In the twenty-fifth year of our exile, at the beginning of the year' etc. [Ezek 40:1].
When they were not worthy to count by themselves, they began to count by the kingdoms, as it says, 'In the second year of Darius' [Hag 1:1], 'in the third year of Cyrus' [Dan 10:1]." Thus far.

And in the Mekhilta, section "In the third month," they said: "They did not wish to count from its building; let them count from its destruction, as it says, 'after the city was smitten' [Ezek 40:1].
They did not wish to count by themselves; let them count by others, as it says, 'in the second year of Nebuchadnezzar' [Dan 2:1], 'in the second year of Darius' [Hag 1:1]." Thus far.
And in the Sifrei, section Be-haalotekha, they said: "'After they came out of the land of Egypt' [Num 9:1]: this teaches you that they counted from the Exodus from Egypt.
They came into the Land, etc.; the Temple was built, etc.; the Temple was destroyed, etc.;
the subjugation grew harsh upon them, and they began to count from their subjugation, as it says, 'in the second year of Nebuchadnezzar,' 'in the second year of Darius.'
And just as they counted the years, so they counted the months." Thus far.
The intent of this last clause is what they said in the Jerusalem Talmud, Rosh Hashanah, that the names of the months and of the angels came up with them from Babylonia: Nisan, Iyyar; Michael, Gabriel.
And in the Mishnah, Rosh Hashanah chapter 1, we learned: "On the first of Nisan is the new year for kings," and it was said [in the Gemara] about this: "What is the practical consequence? For documents,
as we learned, 'Bonds of debt' etc." And from chapter 1 of Tractate Avodah Zarah it appears that for many years after the Destruction the people of the Land of Israel counted from the Destruction,
while the rest of the Diaspora counted by the Greek kingdom, in the manner of the difference between them nowadays regarding the second festival day of the Diaspora.
For R. Yose b. Rabbi taught: "The Persian kingdom in the presence of the Temple, etc.; the kingdom of Herod, 103 [years].
From here on, go out and calculate how many years after the Destruction." Thus far.
And Rashi explained: "From here on, whoever comes to count the order of the Sages and their lives, or a bond of debt, or any event that occurred, should count from the Destruction: now it is such-and-such years since the destruction of the Temple." Thus far.

And there, shortly after, R. Nahman said: "In the Diaspora they count only by the Greek kingdom."
It was also taught thus [in a baraita]: "In the Diaspora they count only by the Greek kingdom."
Ravina said: "Our Mishnah too is precise on this," etc. Also there: "Let us ask the scribe," etc.,
and Rashi explained: "Let us ask the scribe who writes bills of divorce and documents, for he counts by the Greek kingdom."
So too you will see at the end of Seder Olam, chapter 30, that 762 years from the Destruction and 1117 of the Greek kingdom are counted there, ⚠2
although that calculation is not precise, and these words are by no means to be attributed to the author himself,
as we wrote in chapter 19 about this and about the totals written in the Pesikta, section Shelah lekha; see there.
Also in the Epistle of Rav Sherira Gaon, which we cited in the preceding chapter, we saw him set out a number of generations up to the year 1298 of documents [i.e. the Seleucid era], prefacing these words: "according to the Greek reckoning that we customarily use."

So too it will become clear to you from the gloss of the Tosafot in chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah beginning "kafra duqna," which says in the name of Rabbenu Tam:
"If the scribe omitted the [year from the] creation of the world or the number of thousands, there is no concern, for in their days they wrote by the Greek kingdom," etc., up to: "since we now count only from Creation."
It will further become clear from what is found in Gittin, chapter Ha-zoreq, partly in the Mishnah and partly in the Gemara:
[If] he wrote [the date] by an improper kingdom (meaning, as appears from the words of Maimonides, Laws of Divorce, end of chapter 1, one that does not rule over the place where the bill of divorce is written),
or by the kingdom of Media or the kingdom of Greece, or from the building of the Temple or from its destruction, or from the Exodus from Egypt, the bill of divorce is invalid according to R. Meir, on account of "the peace of the government," since it must be dated by the legitimate kingdom.
And although the Ran was precise there, saying: "But [dated] by the count from Creation it is valid, for in honor of Heaven every king will waive his honor,"
surely it appears that this was not customary [in antiquity], for he wrote: "Now they have become accustomed to count from Creation, and they give a reason for this: that [the Sages] instituted this only at a time when it was the way of the nations to count by their kings and they were particular about it.
And they instituted it for bills of divorce and not for documents, because with bills of divorce there is concern for the peace of the government, which is a great matter about which they are particular between a man and his wife;
but for documents they were not concerned, for we say in chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah, 'In the Diaspora they count only by the Greek kingdom.'"
Thus far. Maimonides too in the aforementioned chapter, and the Tur, Even ha-Ezer section 127, with the comments of Karo, and the author of the Arukh, entry lashon, all wrote in the manner stated.

And as appears from chapter 7 of Megillat Ta'anit, they also counted for some time by the Hasmonean priesthood "of God Most High,"
until the Sages decreed, and the mention was removed from the documents, lest they end up on the dunghill, for one must not treat the honor of Heaven lightly.
Thus from Scripture, from the Sages of the Mishnah and Gemara, and from the codifiers, it has become very clear that the count from Creation was not customary among the ancients but was newly introduced among us recently.
I have already mentioned to you passages also from the books of the Hasmoneans, both from the first, which according to the translator for the Christians [Jerome] in his preface to the Book of Samuel was composed in Hebrew, and from the second, which was composed in Greek,
and presumably by different men; from which it became clear that in the days of the [Second] Temple everyone counted by the Greek kingdom.
And not only while they ruled over us and for the sake of the peace of their government,
for even after the yoke was removed from our necks the former custom remained, as we have shown from the words of the Sages we mentioned.
In particular it will become clear to you from the First Book of the Hasmoneans, chapter 14,
in which is cited a memorial record that the Sanhedrin placed on Mount Zion, engraved on a bronze tablet, to make known to mankind the mighty deeds of Simon son of Mattathias the high priest and his benefactions to Israel,
who with God's help brought his people out in joy from the iron furnace of the Greek kings, as we also alluded to at the end of chapter 22.
And there, in chapter 14 of the Hasmoneans, it is stated that those words were written on that tablet on the 8th of Elul, in the year 172 of the Greek kingdom; see there. ⚠3
So we may say that the count from Creation began among our people, as it appears, after Rav Sherira Gaon, less than six hundred years ago.

The reason for this, in my opinion, was that later generations saw that the Greek kingdom had sunk out of the world,
and that we in our servitude do not dwell together under a single kingdom so as to seek its welfare alone,
but are ever moving from kingdom to another people [cf. Ps 105:13]; so they chose to abandon that custom and attach the count to a starting point that would remind us of the kingdom of Heaven,
for every great king and ruler, as the aforementioned Ran says, will waive his honor for its sake.
Or we may say that this mode of counting began at the earliest at the end of the Sages of the Babylonian Gemara, when the order of the fixed calendar was newly introduced among our people.
For you will see in Maimonides, Laws of the Sanctification of the New Moon, chapter 5, how our ancestors always sanctified the months on the basis of sighting [the new moon],
and intercalated the years according to the season and the need, until the days of Abaye and Rava;
for then, owing to the affliction and great devastation of the Land of Israel, the Jews were seized with fear that the permanent court would depart from it, the court on whose shoulder this office rests, the place being the determining factor,
as is explained in chapter 6 of Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer, at the end of Berakhot, and in chapter 1 of Sanhedrin, and as Maimonides of blessed memory also explained in his Sefer ha-Mitzvot, [positive commandment] 153. ⚠4
And the Lord touched the heart of the leaders of the Diaspora, and they arranged for us the calculations of the fixed calendar and intercalation which we calculate at present,
so that throughout our exile we turn out to be fixing [months] and intercalating [years] by the authority of the excellent court that was in the Land.

Now according to the words of Nahmanides in his Hassagot, and of R. Isaac Israeli in his book Yesod Olam, which you will find in chapter 40 below,
the founder of the aforementioned calculation, for the aforementioned reason, was R. Hillel son of R. Judah Nesiah and his court, close to the end of the Sages of the Babylonian Gemara.
And although you will find a contradiction in the words of the aforementioned Israeli about the time of its invention,
for in chapter 5 of Discourse 4 of that book he wrote that it was in the year 4240 from Creation (that is, 811 of documents), ⚠5
and in chapter 9 of it he wrote that it was about three hundred years after the Destruction, and between the one figure and the other there are more than 120 years,
the correct course is to hold to the version of the aforementioned chapter 9; for you have heard Maimonides say that fixing [the months] by sighting continued until the days of Abaye and Rava,
and Rav Sherira Gaon in his aforementioned Epistle, and also Rabad [Abraham ibn Daud] in his Sefer ha-Qabbalah, wrote that Abaye died in the year 649 of documents and Rava in 663.
Also according to what R. Abraham bar Hiyya recorded in his Sefer ha-Ibbur,
Rabbenu Hai of blessed memory wrote in a responsum that from the year 670 of documents, which was 290 after the Destruction, the Sanhedrin and the ordained [scholars] ceased in the Land;
and likewise the author of the Terumot of blessed memory wrote this in the name of Nahmanides, who said it in the name of Rabbenu Hai in the Sefer ha-Zekhut, which we have not been privileged to see shine before us.

But lest you err regarding the cessation of the aforementioned Sanhedrin, thinking that the court of seventy-one and the [Small Sanhedrin] of twenty-three continued among our people until that time,
consider that Maimonides of blessed memory has already explained at the end of chapter 14 of the Laws of the Sanhedrin, in accordance with what follows from chapter 1 of Tractate Avodah Zarah, in these words:
"The Sanhedrin was exiled to ten places, finally to Tiberias, and from there no Great Court has arisen until now; and it is a tradition," etc.
And it is evident from what they said in the Mishnah of chapter 1 of Sanhedrin, "The Great Sanhedrin was of seventy-one," which implies the past.
Moreover, had it existed in the days of those Tannaim, R. Judah would not have disputed, saying seventy:
would they not have entered their chamber to find the count?  Rather, Rabbenu Hai's intent is that not only had the Sanhedrin ceased from that time, as stated, ⚠6
but also all the ordained courts in the Land of Israel, from the chain of the Sanhedrin's ordination, which is the proper ordination, ceased in those days.
And as to the intercalation of the years, we already know that it was performed either by the Sanhedrin itself
or by courts ordained by the authority of the ancient Sanhedrin, which continued from generation to generation in the Land,
as also appears from the words of Maimonides at the beginning of chapter 5 of the Laws of the Sanctification of the New Moon.

And if you find it hard to believe that this R. Judah Nesiah, father of the aforementioned R. Hillel, is the one of whom it is said in chapter Ein ma'amidin and chapter Ha-qometz, "R. Judah Nesiah asked R. Ammi,"
since you will say that he and R. Ammi were of the first generation of Amoraim and his son R. Hillel of the second generation,
whereas Abaye and Rava were of the fourth generation, as all appears from Rabad's Sefer ha-Qabbalah,
you can say that he was his grandson and was named after his grandfather, the aforementioned R. Judah,
inasmuch as the title Nesiah continued throughout the lineage; all the more so as it will become clear to you from the aforementioned Epistle of Rav Sherira that there were sages bearing this title even in the days of the Gaon Rabbenu Saadiah of blessed memory.
Were it not for the words of the aforementioned Rabbenu Hai and Maimonides, we would already have endorsed the other version, of 4240,
for at that time too, owing to the succession of calamities upon our people, the Babylonian Gemara was closed,
according to what Rav Sherira wrote in his Epistle and Rabad at the beginning of [his account of] the order of the Rabbanan Savorai.

All the more so since it seems strange, if the enactment of the aforementioned R. Hillel preceded the closing of the Gemara,
that Rav Ashi, or Amemar after him, did not record any mention of it, since it is a great foundation for the maintenance of our religion. It is also not far-fetched that the intent of Rabbenu Hai and Maimonides in their aforementioned words is only to say that, from the episode of Rava with his fasts onward, from which we learned of the existence of an ordained court in his days, as we shall mention in chapter 40 of Discourse 3,
it is not known from any account in the Gemara that an ordained court still existed in the Land;
not that they ceased from then on by a binding tradition. Accordingly, it is possible that they continued until the time of 4240.
And if we think so, then the difficulty that the Ran of blessed memory, in chapter Ha-sholeah, in the gloss beginning "And some of the Sages," raises against the lenient ones who say that the remission of debts [in the Sabbatical year] does not apply
will necessarily return to him; see there, for I have lifted the potsherd for you, and I shall not now turn my steps from the path.
Be that as it may! In any case the aforementioned enactment was introduced around the time of Rav Ashi, whether before him or after him, as we mentioned.
So it is reasonable to think that amid the upheaval of the times and the turning of those arrangements,
R. Hillel and the aforementioned court likewise saw fit no longer to count by the Greek kingdom and by documents as in former days, and to count from Creation for the reason stated above.

And since they saw that the reckoning of the years from Adam to Solomon's building is explicit in the Torah and the Prophets, except for the doubt about the length of the Egyptian bondage, which is explained by our Sages,
and they found the tradition of 410 years for the First Temple and the statement of R. Yose in Seder Olam chapter 30 and Avodah Zarah chapter 1 indicating that the Second Temple lasted 420 years, all as will be explained with God's help in chapter 35 and other chapters of Discourse 3,
they fixed with them the nails [i.e. firmly established the points] for the count from Creation. And thus, in order, generation after generation, that custom has been kept from then on,
and it will be kept always, until, in the words of the aforementioned Nahmanides and Israeli, "the teacher of righteousness comes" [cf. Hos 10:12; Joel 2:23].
But as it appears, it also continued for a long time after the aforementioned R. Hillel (if indeed the count from Creation began with him, as stated) that in some regions they still counted by documents,
as with Rav Sherira Gaon, who lived within the bounds of Babylonia and wrote the chain of many generations by that count, as is known from his Epistle, which we mentioned in chapter 24.
And the author of the Arukh of blessed memory, a man of Rome, wrote under the word sah, in these words: "They said of Mar Abraham Gaon, who lived in the year 1140 of documents, that he understood the conversation of palm trees." Thus far.
And as for me, I have seen, and I also have in my possession, an old quire of Rashi's commentary on part of Tractate Pesahim,
and at the end of chapter Arvei Pesahim, from which point onward it is the commentary of Rashbam of blessed memory, a French scribe wrote, in these words:

"Completed is Arvei Pesahim, commentary of Rashi of blessed memory, with the help of Heaven, on the eve of the Sabbath, Adar, 1501 of documents."
And Maimonides of blessed memory, who was from Córdoba, a city at the edge of Spain, and in his last days dwelt in Egypt, wrote in the Treatise on Resurrection, in these words:
"In the year 1500 of documents a letter reached us from the lands of Yemen," etc.
And shortly after: "Also in this year, which is 1506 of documents, writings reached us from Babylonia," etc.
And in the Laws of Divorce, end of chapter 1, he wrote, in these words: "Now all Israel are accustomed to count in bills of divorce from Creation or by the reign of Alexander of Macedon, which is the count of documents."
Also among the treasured books of the learned R. Ezra of Fano, resident of Mantua, I saw Maimonides' commentary on Tractate Rosh Hashanah,
and especially on the astronomical passages found there, many of whose words were brought in [his] commentary on the Laws of the Sanctification of the New Moon.
And at its end is written, by the hand of Maimonides' grandson, in these words:

"Our master Moses, peace be upon him, author of this book, was born to his father in the month of Nisan, on the 14th day, in the year 1446 of documents, in the city of Córdoba,
and died in the year 1516 of documents, on Monday, 20 Tevet;
it follows that he illumined the eyes of the Diaspora for seventy years less 83 days.
And my father and master, his son, was born in 1496 of documents and died in 1566 of documents.
And I, David, son of the son of the master my grandfather Rabbi Moses, was born to my father in Egypt in 1534." End of his words.
We should not wonder at these numbers by documents, how they were used for several generations even after the aforementioned R. Hillel and Rav Sherira,
for the displacement of an old custom by a new one, especially in a scattered nation, is difficult without a long span of time;
but in the course of time the dispersions of Israel little by little established and accepted that change.

And since there is no remembrance of former things [Eccl 1:11], many think that the count from Creation came from Sinai and from of old,
whereas in truth it is only new, recently introduced, as we have mentioned.
